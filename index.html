PROBLEM WE IDENTIFIED

Hospitals produce a lot of biomedical waste every day, and it is collected and sorted by hand.

  • Infection & Sharps Hazards

    Staff and sanitation workers can get infected from sharps, blood-soaked items and infectious waste.

  • Human Error & Mixing

    Different types of waste get mixed up because of human mistakes, tiredness or lack of time.

  • Traceability & Compliance Gaps

    It is hard to keep a proper record and follow the waste disposal rules accurately across busy hospital wards.

COMPREHENSIVE ENGINEERING

TECHNICAL APPROACH

Integrating hardware, artificial intelligence, navigation, and tracking

Mobile chassis

Sturdy base platform built for smooth hospital hallway mobility, lift entry, and stable payload transport.

Collection box and hopper

Enclosed intake receptacle that receives mixed ward waste directly from the ward bin.

Gate and slow conveyor

Controlled metering gate releases items steadily onto a low-speed conveyor to avoid jamming.

AI camera

Overhead optical sensor positioned above the conveyor for continuous visual recognition of items.

2 robotic hands

Dual articulated manipulators engineered to pick and place categorized medical waste items into assigned chutes.

Gravity chutes

Passive downward channels that guide sorted items cleanly into storage compartments with zero extra motors.

4 sealed compartments

Four isolated, odor-sealed and leak-proof internal bins matching standard biomedical color codes.

LiDAR sensors

360-degree laser range finding for real-time obstacle avoidance, doorway clearance, and 2D mapping.

Weight sensors

Integrated load cells to measure collected waste mass per ward and monitor compartment capacity.

PRACTICAL IMPLEMENTATION

WHY IT IS FEASIBLE

Grounded in proven components and realistic hospital infrastructure

EXISTING TECHNOLOGY

Mobile robots, conveyors, robotic arms, camera-based AI and LiDAR navigation already exist as components.

SIMPLIFIED MOBILITY

No stairs. The robot uses the hospital lift to move seamlessly across all hospital floors.

LOWER COMPLEXITY

Two robotic hands instead of four, drastically simplifying control algorithms and mechanical failure points.

GRAVITY-BASED SORTING

Gravity chutes require no extra motors, reducing total robot power draw and moving parts.

HOSPITAL COMPATIBILITY

Hospitals already have lifts, bins and waste-management departments ready for integration.
